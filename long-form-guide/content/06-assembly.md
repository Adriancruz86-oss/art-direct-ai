# Part VI: Assemble and Finish the Manuscript

Chapters written separately do not automatically become a book. Assembly reveals duplicated openings, missing transitions, conflicting definitions, inconsistent formatting, and changes in voice that were invisible inside individual files.

## 1. Establish version control

Version control means knowing which manuscript is current, what changed, and how to recover an earlier state.

Use:

- One current assembly file
- Numbered chapter files
- Dates or version numbers
- A changelog for major revisions
- An archive for superseded files
- Regular backups in a separate location

Do not overwrite the only recoverable copy. Do not use "final" as a version system.

## 2. Create a chapter-status dashboard

Track each chapter through:

- Brief approved
- Draft complete
- Sources verified
- Structural revision complete
- Voice revision complete
- Human review complete
- Final proof complete

The dashboard exposes false completion. A chapter with polished sentences but unverified sources is not finished.

## 3. Assemble approved chapters

Combine only approved chapter versions. Preserve heading levels and intentional section breaks. Add temporary page breaks between chapters, then remove layout artifacts after the manuscript format is chosen.

Create an assembly log containing the source filename and version for every imported chapter.

## 4. Repair transitions

Read the final two pages of one chapter and the first two pages of the next. Check:

- Does the next chapter reintroduce the whole book?
- Does it assume information the prior chapter removed?
- Does the emotional or argumentative movement continue?
- Are repeated examples now adjacent?
- Does the transition promise the correct next step?

Write transitions after assembly, not in isolation.

## 5. Complete front and back matter

Depending on the book, prepare:

- Title and copyright pages
- Dedication or epigraph with verified permission
- Contents
- Introduction or preface
- Notes
- Bibliography
- Acknowledgments
- Discussion or reflection guide
- About the author
- Resource and help information for sensitive topics

[BIBLICAL PROJECT]
State the Bible translation policy. Identify when quotations come from a named translation. Include source and permissions information required by the publisher. Explain that the book offers general formation and does not replace pastoral, medical, mental-health, or legal care.

## 6. Prepare the beta-reader edition

Give beta readers a stable manuscript and a defined role. Ask questions tied to the book's promise:

- Where did your attention weaken?
- Which chapter felt repetitive or misplaced?
- Which term remained unclear?
- Where did the tone feel shaming, childish, vague, or performative?
- Which application felt realistic or unrealistic?
- What did the book seem to promise but never deliver?

Do not ask every reader to solve every problem.

## 7. Triage feedback

Sort feedback into:

- Clear defect
- Pattern reported by several readers
- Reader preference
- Conflict with the book promise
- New idea for another project

The author decides. Feedback is evidence about reader experience, not a vote on every sentence.

## 8. Complete the final audit

Before export, verify:

- Title, subtitle, author name, and metadata
- Contents and page references
- Heading hierarchy
- Chapter order and numbering
- Cross-references
- Quotations and permissions
- Notes and bibliography
- Scripture references and translation labels
- Spelling of names and defined terms
- Repeated or missing text at file joins
- Images, captions, and accessibility text if used
- Disclosure choices required by the publisher or author
- Clean source files and recoverable backups

## 9. Export and inspect

Export the manuscript to its delivery format. Never assume a successful export is a correct export.

Inspect:

- First and last page
- Every chapter opening
- Pages with tables, callouts, or long quotations
- Widows, orphans, clipping, and unexpected blank pages
- Headers, footers, and page numbers
- Embedded links and bookmarks
- Searchable text and readable characters

The book is complete when the artifact has been inspected, not when the export button has been pressed.
