# Part I: Build the Foundation

A long manuscript begins before the first chapter. It begins when the author decides what kind of promise the book will make and what evidence will count as keeping it.

The foundation does not need to predict every sentence. It needs to keep future decisions from contradicting one another. It gives the book a center of gravity.

## 1. Write the book promise

The book promise is one sentence describing the experience or change the manuscript will deliver for a specific reader.

A useful promise includes:

- The reader
- The problem, desire, or experience
- The form of change
- The book's distinct approach

Weak promise: "This book is about becoming a better man."

Stronger promise: "This book helps young men ages 13-21 understand biblical manhood as a life of identity, responsibility, strength under control, service, and faithful action - through Scripture in context, honest modern situations, and practical choices."

The stronger version is still provisional. Terms such as "biblical manhood" and "strength under control" require definition. But it can guide architecture. It suggests what belongs and exposes what does not.

[FICTION]
A fiction promise describes the kind of experience the story will deliver. It includes genre, central tension, emotional movement, and the question that keeps the reader turning pages. "A grieving apprentice must cross a flooded city with the rival she blames for her brother's death" gives the story more control than "a fantasy adventure about forgiveness."

[NONFICTION]
A nonfiction promise describes a reader transformation. Avoid promising an outcome the book cannot guarantee. "Understand and practice" is more responsible than "become." The manuscript can offer principles, tools, and challenges. It cannot control the reader's life.

[EXERCISE]
Write three versions of the promise. Make one practical, one emotional, and one narrowly specific. Compare them. Choose the version that gives you the clearest standard for what to exclude.

## 2. Define the reader precisely

"Everyone" is not a reader profile. A book may reach people outside its intended audience, but the prose needs someone to face.

Record:

- Approximate age and life stage
- Existing knowledge
- Existing beliefs or expectations
- Immediate problems and private fears
- What language feels natural
- What language feels false, academic, childish, or patronizing
- What the reader is ready to do now
- What the reader is not yet prepared to carry

[BIBLICAL PROJECT]
Ages 13-21 is a wide range. A thirteen-year-old may depend on parents for nearly every major decision. A twenty-one-year-old may be working full time, studying away from home, dating seriously, caring for family members, or living independently. The project should choose a primary reader, such as a sixteen- or seventeen-year-old son, then use sidebars or examples that expand upward and downward.

Writing to the midpoint prevents the voice from becoming childish for older readers or inaccessible for younger ones. Scenarios can vary by stage:

- Younger: school, siblings, chores, sports, online behavior, early friendships
- Middle: driving, work, dating, academic pressure, independence, private habits
- Older: employment, college, money, serious relationships, leadership, living choices

The book should never confuse directness with contempt. Young readers can handle difficult ideas when the writer defines terms, respects their intelligence, and connects abstraction to life.

## 3. Define the reader transformation

The transformation is the path from the reader's starting state to a more capable state.

Use four columns:

1. **Before:** What does the reader currently misunderstand, avoid, or lack?
2. **Understand:** What should the reader be able to explain?
3. **Practice:** What should the reader be able to do?
4. **Continue:** What habits or questions should remain after the final page?

For the biblical-manhood project:

- Before: manhood is confused with age, dominance, toughness, sexual experience, income, or approval.
- Understand: biblical manhood is rooted in identity and expressed through responsibility, courage, restraint, service, integrity, and faithfulness.
- Practice: the reader can identify a responsible action in a real situation and take it.
- Continue: the reader has reflection habits, trusted adults, Scripture study practices, and a clearer standard for growth.

This transformation is an editorial test. If a chapter is interesting but does not move the reader along the path, it may belong in another book.

## 4. Build the master project bible

The project bible is the book's external memory. It stores stable decisions so they do not have to be reconstructed from an AI conversation.

The first version should contain:

### Identity

- Working title and subtitle
- Book promise
- Primary and secondary readers
- Genre or category
- Target length
- Intended reading level and emotional register

### Content boundaries

- What the book covers
- What it explicitly does not cover
- Terms that require definition
- Claims that require sources
- Sensitive areas requiring expert review

### Structure

- Parts or acts
- Current chapter list
- The function of each part
- Major dependencies
- Planned ending

### Voice

- Point of view
- Degree of directness
- Typical sentence length and rhythm
- Vocabulary level
- Humor policy
- Treatment of uncertainty
- Preferred and prohibited habits

### Evidence or continuity

- Source hierarchy for nonfiction
- World rules for fiction
- Timeline anchors
- Character or terminology records
- Citation policy

### Decision status

- Approved decisions
- Provisional decisions
- Open questions
- Rejected approaches and why they were rejected

The bible should be compact enough to review before every major session. The manuscript contains the full writing. The bible contains the decisions needed to produce it.

[WARNING]
Do not let the project bible become a warehouse. If you paste every brainstorm into it, the stable decisions disappear. Keep exploration in working notes. Promote only approved decisions into the bible.

## 5. Create a usable voice sheet

Voice becomes controllable when it is described through behavior rather than praise.

Instead of "clear and engaging," record instructions such as:

- Address the reader directly when asking for action, but do not use the reader's name.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Define a theological or technical term before using it as shorthand.
- Use one strong example instead of three shallow examples.
- Admit uncertainty directly.
- Do not end every section with inspiration.
- Avoid "in today's world," "it's important to remember," and other generic transition phrases.
- Vary paragraph length, but keep most paragraphs under 140 words.
- Use questions to open reflection, not as decorative hooks.

Add short examples of language that sounds right and language that does not. The examples can come from the author's existing writing. If the author is developing a new voice, create samples, revise them manually, and approve only the sentences that feel truthful.

[BIBLICAL PROJECT]
The voice should be serious without becoming severe, masculine without performance, and pastoral without pretending to be the reader's pastor. It should distinguish challenge from shame. "You are responsible for your next choice" gives agency. "A real man would never struggle with this" punishes honesty and encourages concealment.

The voice sheet should prohibit:

- Mocking vulnerability
- Treating emotion as weakness
- Equating domination with leadership
- Promising that right behavior guarantees comfort, romance, success, or safety
- Using Scripture as a slogan without context
- Speaking as if every family, church, body, or developmental experience is identical

## 6. Separate decisions from open questions

AI is excellent at generating possibilities. That strength becomes a problem when possibilities quietly enter the manuscript as decisions.

Maintain three lists:

### Approved

The book will define courage as faithful action in the presence of fear, not the absence of fear.

### Provisional

The book may use a three-part progression: identity, practice, responsibility.

### Open

Should the chapter on work come before or after the chapter on discipline?

Every drafting session should know which category it is working in. Do not ask for polished prose around an unresolved structural question. Ask for options, compare them, decide, and update the log.

## 7. Establish source standards before drafting

Nonfiction needs a source hierarchy. Decide what kinds of sources can support which kinds of claims.

A practical hierarchy may be:

1. Primary source or original text
2. Peer-reviewed or authoritative reference
3. Reputable scholarly or professional synthesis
4. Credible journalism or institutional publication
5. Personal account, used as an example rather than universal proof
6. AI-generated suggestion, treated only as a lead to verify

[NONFICTION]
For each factual claim, record the claim, source, source type, location, verification date, and status. A bibliography assembled from memory at the end cannot repair months of untracked drafting.

[BIBLICAL PROJECT]
The Bible is the primary text, but interpretation still requires decisions. Record:

- Passage reference
- Translation used for any direct quotation
- Immediate literary context
- Relevant historical or canonical context used by the author
- Observation from the text
- Interpretation adopted by the author
- Theological tradition or source informing that interpretation, when relevant
- Modern application
- Human reviewer and review status

Choose a translation policy. Options include using one primary translation throughout, comparing translations only when the wording matters, or paraphrasing while citing the reference. Do not blend wording from several translations into a quotation. Do not accept a model's memory of a verse. Open the source and check it.

## 8. Set boundaries for sensitive guidance

A book for young readers may touch mental health, abuse, sexuality, addiction, medical concerns, legal issues, family conflict, or spiritual authority. Decide what the book can responsibly do.

It may:

- Offer general principles
- Encourage the reader to seek trustworthy help
- Describe warning signs carefully
- Direct the reader toward qualified adults and professionals
- Acknowledge that some situations require protection rather than endurance

It should not:

- Diagnose the reader
- Tell a minor to conceal danger
- Treat abuse as a character-building opportunity
- Replace medical, legal, or mental-health care
- Promise spiritual certainty about a specific personal crisis

These boundaries belong in the project bible and the final review checklist.

## 9. Build the fiction foundation

[FICTION]
Record the following before expanding the outline:

- **Premise:** Who wants what, what opposes them, and what happens if they fail?
- **Genre promise:** What pleasures and expectations bring the reader to this kind of story?
- **Point of view:** Who can perceive and narrate each scene?
- **Character engine:** What does the protagonist want, need, fear, and misunderstand?
- **Opposition:** Who or what actively makes the goal difficult?
- **World rules:** What can and cannot happen?
- **Ending target:** What choice, cost, revelation, or change completes the story?

Do not ask AI to produce a detailed plot before these elements agree. A plot is not a pile of events. It is pressure applied to desire over time.

## 10. Build the nonfiction foundation

[NONFICTION]
Record:

- **Thesis:** The central claim the book will establish
- **Reader problem:** The practical or intellectual problem the reader brings
- **Argument path:** The sequence of ideas required to reach the conclusion
- **Evidence standard:** What counts as support
- **Counterarguments:** Which reasonable objections deserve treatment
- **Application:** What the reader can do with the understanding
- **Exclusions:** Questions the book acknowledges but will not settle

A nonfiction chapter should not exist only because the topic is interesting. It needs a place in the argument or transformation.

## 11. Approve the foundation

Before moving to architecture, perform a foundation review:

- Can the promise fit in one sentence?
- Does the primary reader feel like a person rather than a demographic?
- Is the transformation observable?
- Are key terms defined or scheduled for definition?
- Are content boundaries explicit?
- Are source and quotation rules recorded?
- Are sensitive areas flagged for human review?
- Does the voice sheet describe behavior?
- Are approved decisions separated from open questions?
- Can the author explain why this book should exist?

If the answers are weak, do not solve the problem with more chapters. Repair the foundation.
