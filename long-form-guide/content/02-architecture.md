# Part II: Architect 60,000 Words

Architecture turns a good intention into a reading experience. It decides when the reader learns something, when a question opens, when pressure increases, when a concept returns at a deeper level, and what must be true before the ending can work.

AI can propose structures quickly. It cannot know which structure best expresses your purpose until you define the criteria and choose among the options.

## 1. Choose the book's large movement

Begin with three to five parts. Each part should change the reader's position.

A nonfiction progression might move through:

1. Reframing the problem
2. Building the foundation
3. Practicing the principles
4. Applying them under pressure
5. Continuing without the book

A novel might move through:

1. Disruption
2. Pursuit
3. Escalation
4. Crisis
5. Consequence and resolution

Part names are not enough. Write a function statement:

> By the end of Part II, the reader understands that responsibility grows from identity rather than from performance, and can distinguish responsibility from control.

or:

> By the end of Act II, Mara has achieved the goal she thought would solve her problem, but the cost exposes the belief that has been driving her.

The function statement lets you test whether a proposed chapter belongs.

## 2. Build 20-30 chapters

A 60,000-word book often works well with 20-30 chapters. The range is not mandatory, but it creates units small enough to control and large enough to develop an idea or dramatic movement.

One workable nonfiction model is 24 chapters:

| Part | Function | Chapters | Word budget |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| I | Name and reframe the problem | 4 | 8,000 |
| II | Establish identity and foundations | 5 | 12,000 |
| III | Build daily practices | 6 | 15,000 |
| IV | Apply principles under pressure | 6 | 16,000 |
| V | Integrate, continue, and conclude | 3 | 9,000 |
| **Total** |  | **24** | **60,000** |

The average is 2,500 words per chapter, but averages should not flatten the book. The opening may be shorter. A difficult central chapter may be longer. The final chapter may need room for synthesis without introducing new arguments.

[FICTION]
A 24-chapter novel can use four six-chapter movements, three eight-chapter acts, or an irregular design driven by turning points. Do not force every act to the same length. Track the function and pressure of each movement.

[NONFICTION]
Nonfiction architecture follows dependencies. If the reader must understand identity before responsibility, and responsibility before leadership, the order should reflect that. A list of popular topics is not an argument.

## 3. Give every chapter one primary job

A chapter may do several things, but it needs one primary function.

Useful functions include:

- Define a key term
- Correct a misconception
- Establish evidence
- Introduce a practice
- Complicate an earlier idea
- Answer an objection
- Apply a principle under pressure
- Force a character choice
- Reveal information
- Reverse the apparent direction
- Pay off an earlier setup
- Prepare the ending

Write the function as a verb statement: "distinguish courage from fearlessness" is stronger than "courage."

If a chapter has two unrelated primary verbs, split it or decide which job serves the promise.

## 4. Map dependencies

A dependency is something the reader must know, believe, feel, or remember before a later chapter can work.

Create a simple map:

- Chapter 3 defines responsibility.
- Chapter 7 applies responsibility to work.
- Chapter 12 complicates responsibility when authority is unjust.
- Chapter 19 applies responsibility to leadership.
- Chapter 23 asks the reader to design a personal rule of life.

Now the repeated term has development. Without the map, the chapters may restate the same definition five times.

[FICTION]
Track information dependencies and emotional dependencies separately. A reader may know that a character lied before another character discovers it. A reconciliation may be logically possible but emotionally unearned because the necessary choices have not occurred.

[NONFICTION]
Track term dependencies, evidence dependencies, and practice dependencies. If a later exercise requires a distinction the manuscript never taught, the architecture has a gap.

## 5. Assign word budgets

Word budgets help you allocate attention before prose makes every idea feel indispensable.

For each chapter, record:

- Minimum viable length
- Target length
- Maximum length before review
- Number of major moves
- Space reserved for examples or scenes
- Space reserved for application or reflection

Example:

| Element | Words |
| --- | ---: |
| Opening situation | 250 |
| Scripture or source context | 450 |
| Core principle | 650 |
| Contemporary example | 500 |
| Objection or complication | 300 |
| Practical action | 250 |
| Reflection questions and transition | 100 |
| **Target** | **2,500** |

This is not a formula every chapter must copy. It is an accounting tool. If the core principle takes 1,400 words, something else must change.

## 6. Create chapter briefs

A chapter brief is the contract between architecture and drafting. It should fit on one or two pages.

Include:

- Chapter number and working title
- Primary job
- Reader state at the beginning
- Reader state at the end
- Required prior knowledge
- Key claim or dramatic change
- Supporting evidence, sources, scenes, or examples
- Terms to define or preserve
- Material that must not enter this chapter
- Planned opening and closing movement
- Word budget
- Continuity or safety checks
- Open author decisions

The brief should be approved before prose. AI may help generate options for the brief, but the author decides which job, evidence, and movement belong.

[BIBLICAL PROJECT]
A chapter brief for **Strength Under Control** might contain:

- **Primary job:** Replace the idea that strength means domination with strength governed by love, wisdom, and responsibility.
- **Beginning state:** The reader may associate manhood with physical power, emotional hardness, social control, or winning.
- **Ending state:** The reader can identify restraint as an active use of strength and name one situation where restraint requires courage.
- **Scripture plan:** References selected and checked by the author; context notes recorded in the citation ledger.
- **Modern situation:** A friend humiliates the reader online and the reader has the power to retaliate publicly.
- **Complication:** Restraint does not mean passivity in the face of abuse or danger.
- **Application:** Pause, seek wise counsel, choose a response that protects truth and people rather than pride.
- **Human review:** Theology, youth suitability, and safety review required.
- **Exclusions:** Do not diagnose anger disorders or give legal advice.

The brief controls what the chapter is allowed to become.

## 7. Stress-test the structure

Before drafting, try to break the outline.

### The promise test

For every chapter, finish: "This chapter helps keep the book's promise by..." If the answer is vague, the chapter may be ornamental.

### The repetition test

List each chapter's primary claim or change in one sentence. Highlight sentences that are essentially identical. Decide whether the repetition is developmental, reinforcing, or accidental.

### The dependency test

Ask what the reader must already understand. Confirm that the prerequisite appears earlier and is strong enough to support the later use.

### The missing-middle test

Many outlines have a strong opening and ending but a middle made of related topics. The middle should increase difficulty. Principles meet resistance. Characters pay costs. Objections grow stronger. Applications become less obvious.

### The escalation test

[FICTION]
Does pressure increase? Do choices narrow? Do consequences accumulate? Does each major success create or expose another problem?

[NONFICTION]
Does the argument become more demanding? Does the reader move from understanding to practice, from simple cases to complicated ones, and from private choices to relational or public responsibility?

### The ending test

Does the ending complete the book's movement rather than merely summarize the table of contents? The ending should return responsibility to the reader. It should not introduce a second book's worth of claims.

## 8. Use AI to test architecture without surrendering it

Give the assistant the book promise, reader profile, part functions, chapter list, and evaluation criteria. Ask for diagnosis, not replacement.

A useful request:

> Review this architecture as a skeptical developmental editor. Identify duplicated chapter functions, missing dependencies, abrupt difficulty changes, chapters that do not advance the promise, and places where the middle loses momentum. Do not rewrite the outline. Return a table with the issue, evidence from the outline, consequence for the reader, and two possible author decisions.

The output is an audit. It does not become the new outline automatically.

[WARNING]
When an assistant recommends adding a chapter, ask what existing chapter should shrink, move, or disappear. Uncontrolled addition is the easiest way for a 60,000-word plan to become an 85,000-word draft with no stronger center.

## 9. Running nonfiction architecture: biblical manhood

The following progression is an example, not a theological prescription.

### Part I: What manhood is not

1. The question beneath the stereotypes
2. Manhood is not age, image, dominance, or approval
3. Identity before performance
4. Responsibility without control

### Part II: Foundations

5. Strength under control
6. Courage in the presence of fear
7. Integrity when nobody is watching
8. Discipline and the direction of desire
9. Wisdom, counsel, and teachability

### Part III: Daily practice

10. Work and stewardship
11. Speech and truth
12. Friendship and loyalty
13. Family responsibility
14. Technology and private choices
15. Caring for body and mind

### Part IV: Pressure and relationships

16. Anger, conflict, and repair
17. Attraction, sexuality, and dignity
18. Dating and boundaries
19. Failure, confession, and change
20. Authority, injustice, and conscience
21. Leadership as service

### Part V: Continuing

22. Choosing community and mentors
23. Building a rule of life
24. Becoming faithful over time

This architecture contains 24 chapters and a visible movement: false definitions, foundations, ordinary practice, difficult pressure, and continuing formation.

It also reveals editorial questions:

- Is "caring for body and mind" within the author's competence, and which reviewers are needed?
- Does the sexuality chapter require separate versions or sidebars for different ages?
- How will the book address readers without a safe father, church, or family environment?
- Which tradition informs the treatment of authority, leadership, and sexuality?
- How will disagreement be acknowledged without turning the book into a survey of every position?

Those questions belong in the decision log before drafting.

## 10. Compact fiction architecture

[FICTION]
For comparison, imagine a novel about Mara, a teenage mapmaker in a city that floods a little higher each year. She believes her missing brother abandoned the family. When she discovers his coded maps, she must work with the rival apprentice she blames for his disappearance.

The architectural spine might be:

- **Part I - Disruption:** A flood destroys Mara's workshop; the coded map appears.
- **Part II - Pursuit:** Mara and the rival follow the map through closed districts and uncover her brother's investigation.
- **Part III - Escalation:** Evidence suggests the city's flood defenses are being manipulated; Mara's certainty about her brother begins to fail.
- **Part IV - Crisis:** Stopping the next flood requires trusting the rival and revealing a family secret that will destroy her public identity.
- **Part V - Consequence:** Mara chooses truth over vindication, saves part of the city, and accepts that repair is not the same as restoration.

The project bible records world rules, map logic, flood timeline, character knowledge, and the truth about the brother. Chapter briefs control when each clue appears and what changes because of it.

## 11. Approve the architecture

Do not begin full drafting until you can answer yes:

- Does every part change the reader's position?
- Does every chapter have one primary job?
- Do the chapters form a sequence rather than a shelf of topics?
- Are important dependencies mapped?
- Is the middle more demanding than the beginning?
- Does the word budget total approximately 60,000 words?
- Are fiction continuity or nonfiction source needs visible?
- Are sensitive chapters flagged for qualified review?
- Does the ending fulfill the promise?
- Can each chapter now receive a precise chapter brief?

Architecture will change during drafting. That is not failure. The goal is to make every change visible, deliberate, and connected to the book's promise.
