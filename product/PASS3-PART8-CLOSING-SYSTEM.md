# 31 — Stop Doing This / Do This Instead

## STOP: "Make it beautiful."
**DO:** Decide what deserves attention.

Beauty is not a useful production instruction by itself. Name the focal point, rendering ceiling, mood and what should remain simple.

---

## STOP: Letting every surface become detailed.
**DO:** Give the page a detail budget.

Spend detail where the story needs it. Simplify supporting hair, fabric, foliage, props and surfaces.

---

## STOP: Asking for "watercolor style."
**DO:** Describe how the medium behaves.

Specify visible paper, transparent wash, dry pencil, broken edges, flat areas, sparse marks or whatever actually defines the project's visual language.

---

## STOP: Designing characters one page at a time.
**DO:** Lock identity before page one.

Reference silhouette, age, proportions, hair mass, face system, clothing and recurring companions.

---

## STOP: Comparing only the face.
**DO:** Check the whole identity system.

Age, body proportion, hair, clothing, shoes, companion scale and rendering style can drift while the face still looks "close."

---

## STOP: Generating a picture and finding room for words later.
**DO:** Design the text space into the composition.

Tell the model where the illustration should remain quiet. Typeset the actual words normally.

---

## STOP: Making every page a polished full scene.
**DO:** Vary distance, density and scale.

Use wide views, medium views, close-ups, details, spots and quiet paper to create rhythm.

---

## STOP: Regenerating the entire page because one thing broke.
**DO:** Diagnose and repair the smallest useful area.

Preserve every correct decision you can.

---

## STOP: Adding action because a static pose feels boring.
**DO:** Use composition for variety when movement does not matter.

Walking and running create anatomy risk. Movement should serve the manuscript.

---

## STOP: Telling the model "don't make it look AI."
**DO:** Name the visual behaviors you reject.

No glossy eyes. No rim light. No ornate sky. No unnecessary foliage. No over-rendered hair. Whatever belongs on **your** project's refusal list.

---

## STOP: Approving an image because it is impressive.
**DO:** Ask whether it serves the book.

Not every page gets to be the cover.

# 32 — From Idea to Finished Illustration

This is the final workflow. Keep the finished spread visually simple: one path, six stops, brief copy, substantial empty paper.

## 1. CLEAR IDEA

Start with the manuscript beat.

What must the reader understand or feel on this page?

Do not begin with style adjectives.

**Output:** one clear visual intention.

---

## 2. DETAILED DIRECTION

Translate the intention into decisions:

- subject + action;
- composition;
- character locks;
- medium/surface;
- detail ceiling;
- mood;
- restraint.

**Output:** a directed prompt, not a wish for a pretty picture.

---

## 3. GENERATE & REVIEW

Do not ask only whether you like the result.

Compare it against:

- manuscript;
- canonical references;
- model pages;
- neighboring pages.

Complete:

> **The image fails because __________.**

Or, if it works, stop changing it.

---

## 4. REFINE & ITERATE

Change the variable responsible for the failure.

Repair locally when possible.

Do not reopen successful decisions without a reason.

**Output:** a controlled revision rather than a random new attempt.

---

## 5. FINALIZE & PREPARE

Run the final illustration check:

- story;
- character;
- style;
- composition;
- text space;
- anatomy/artifacts.

Remove generated text and placeholder layout effects.

**Output:** approved illustration asset.

---

## 6. ADD TO YOUR BOOK

Typeset normally.

Place the art in the real page layout.

Check trim, gutter, sequence and production requirements.

View it beside the surrounding pages.

**Output:** a book page — not merely an image.

---

## The loop

The six steps are not a promise that every page works on the first attempt.

They give you somewhere useful to return when it does not.

If the character drifted, return to references.

If the page is crowded, return to composition/detail.

If the style drifted, return to visual language.

If one hand broke, repair the hand.

Do not return automatically to the beginning.

# 33 — Closing Page

Keep this page almost empty.

Suggested final copy:

# AI CAN MAKE THE MARKS.

## YOU STILL HAVE TO MAKE THE DECISIONS.

A generator can give you options faster than any illustrator has ever had them.

That does not remove the need for taste, restraint, continuity, pacing, judgment or editing.

Those are the parts that make the illustrations belong to **your book**.

**Art direct it.**

Optional restrained illustration accent only: canonical boy and dog as a small pencil/colored-pencil spot, or a few working tools/reference sheets. No elaborate closing scene.
