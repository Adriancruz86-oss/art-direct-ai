# PAGE 9 — TOO MUCH / JUST RIGHT / TOO LITTLE

# DETAIL IS A DIAL, NOT A SWITCH.

**TOO MUCH**  
Several areas compete for attention. Hair, clothing, background, and surfaces are rendered simply because they can be.

**JUST RIGHT**  
The focal idea gets enough information to read clearly. Supporting areas are simplified.

**TOO LITTLE**  
Important form, emotion, or story information disappears.

> **Restraint means knowing what deserves finishing.**

# PAGE 10 — BACKGROUND DETAIL

# A BACKGROUND HAS A JOB.
## IT DOESN'T HAVE TO SHOW OFF.

A background usually needs to establish place, support mood, provide useful story information, and help the main action read.

Ask for a child beside a lake and AI may add reeds, mountains, reflected clouds, dozens of trees, rocks, flowers, birds, and texture across every inch of water.

The result may be beautiful.

It may also turn a quiet moment into a landscape demonstration.

Instead of:

> "Beautiful detailed lakeside background."

Direct the function:

> "Distant shoreline suggested with two or three broad shapes. Water mostly open wash and visible paper. Only a few marks near the dock. No decorative foreground plants. Sky quiet and secondary."

## BACKGROUND TEST

Cover the characters.

> **Does the background still look like it's trying to win an illustration award by itself?**

If so, it may be doing too much.

# PAGE 11 — FABRIC & CLOTHING

# DOES THE STORY CARE ABOUT THE SWEATER?

AI can turn a plain hoodie into a showcase of seams, stitching, drawstrings, hardware, folds, fabric texture, and pattern.

None of those details is automatically wrong.

But every approved detail becomes something you may have to reproduce across the book.

## CLOTHING USUALLY HAS THREE JOBS

**1. Identify the character.**  
**2. Explain the pose.**  
**3. Contribute a useful shape or color.**

That is enough for most pages.

Useful direction:

> "Plain red hoodie. No logo, pattern, decorative stitching, fabric texture, or extra trim. Only enough folds to explain the pose."

Keep folds that explain a bent elbow, seated leg, twist, or hanging fabric.

Remove folds whose only job is realistic cloth simulation.

> **Would a traditional illustrator redraw this much clothing detail on twenty pages?**

If probably not, simplify it now.

### ART / LAYOUT
Let the retained fabric comparison dominate. Copy should sit in quiet margins.

### ASSET
Source comp #9 — KEEP ART. Final anatomy check only.

# PAGE 12 — FACES

# THE REST OF THE PAGE CAN BE LOOSE.
## ONE POLISHED FACE CAN BREAK THE STYLE.

Watch for:

- glossy catchlights;
- perfectly smooth skin;
- symmetrical eyes;
- individual lashes;
- carefully graded cheeks;
- shiny lips;
- detailed teeth;
- highly rendered hair around the face.

Instead of asking only who the character is, set the rendering ceiling:

> "Facial features indicated with very few pencil marks. Simple eyes without glossy highlights. Skin mostly flat with a faint wash. No individual eyelashes. Mouth as a simple mark. Preserve small hand-drawn asymmetries."

Expression does not require rendering.

A head tilt, eyebrow angle, small mouth change, posture, or direction of gaze may carry the whole emotion.

## THUMBNAIL TEST

Shrink the page to roughly its printed viewing size.

If the expression still reads, detail visible only at extreme zoom probably doesn't need to be there.

### ART / LAYOUT
Use the strongest source #13 face comparison. The approved/good face may receive a local hair/detail simplification only if needed.

### ASSET

# PAGES 13–14 — THE QUIET PAGE

# AI WANTS TO FILL.
## YOU DON'T HAVE TO LET IT.

One of the strongest decisions in a children's book can be a page where almost nothing happens visually.

A small character.

One meaningful object.

A few pencil marks.

A large area of untouched paper.

That quiet can communicate loneliness, relief, waiting, wonder, distance, uncertainty — or simply a pause before the next event.

## EMPTY DOESN'T MEAN UNFINISHED

When a generator sees open space, it often tries to help.

A blank wall gets pictures.  
An empty field gets flowers.  
A quiet sky gets birds.  
A desk gets books, pencils, plants, and toys.

Every addition may be plausible.

Together, they destroy the pause.

Useful direction:

> "Keep most of the page as untouched warm paper."

> "One small figure low on the page. No decorative background objects."

> "Allow the wash to fade completely into raw paper."

> "Do not balance the empty side with another object."

## THE RESTRAINT RULE

Before approving a page, ask:

> **What can I remove without losing the story?**

Remove it mentally.

If the page gets stronger, it probably didn't belong there.

# PAGE 15 — PART III OPENER

# MAKE A CHARACTER.
## NOT 30 VERSIONS OF ONE.

Character consistency does not begin when page two looks wrong.

It begins before page one.

AI can reproduce a general idea — brown-haired boy, red hoodie, spotted dog — while quietly changing the actual character every time.

The face gets older.  
The head gets narrower.  
The hair grows.  
The hoodie changes.  
The shoes become boots.  
The dog gets taller.  
The spots move.

Each page can look plausible by itself.

Together, you have a cast of cousins.

> **Lock identity before you start asking for poses.**

### ART / LAYOUT
Use one canonical boy/dog drawing beside 2–3 intentionally obvious drift silhouettes/details. Do not make this another full consistency lesson; it is the section opener.
