## Cross-book visual corrections

### 1. Stop using generated typography as final typography
Every retained spread should be rebuilt with real text. Image-generated headings, captions, checklists, page numbers, measurements, and labels are placeholders only.

### 2. The “good” art must obey a stricter ceiling
Across all retained good examples:
- fewer individual hair strands,
- no glossy eyes,
- plain clothing unless detail is narratively required,
- fewer small plants/rocks/props,
- more raw paper,
- flatter or quieter skies,
- no cinematic rim light,
- no decorative glow,
- no “perfect sunset” unless the story specifically requires one.

### 3. Use pose simplicity as a quality-control tool
Production note now locked:
- avoid walking/running poses unless movement matters to the story,
- prefer clean silhouettes when a static pose teaches the same lesson,
- clearly separate legs/feet in full-body poses,
- if a pose introduces anatomy risk without adding story value, simplify it.

### 4. Do not let the book become the thing it criticizes
Several later comps became too clean, boxed, dense, and “everything is important.” Final layout should alternate:
- dense tool/reference page,
- normal lesson page,
- quiet visual page,
- worked example.

### 5. Kill fake physical-book effects
Generated center seams, photographed-book mockups, page curls, or fake binding shadows should be removed unless the lesson is specifically about a physical gutter. Final PDF pages should be designed flat.

### 6. Art consistency check
The recurring boy/dog examples drift subtly across the generated comps. In the final product, either:
- choose one canonical boy/dog reference and rebuild retained examples against it, or
- state clearly that some chapters use separate demonstration characters.

Do not accidentally teach consistency using an inconsistent recurring cast.

### 7. Production claims need a fact-check pass
Before final typesetting, verify:
- bleed values,
- safe-zone recommendations,
- gutter guidance,
- 300 dpi wording,
- CMYK/RGB requirements,
- crop-mark requirements,
- PDF/export requirements,
- font embedding/outlining guidance.

Printer/platform rules vary. The guide should distinguish “common practice” from “required by your printer.”
