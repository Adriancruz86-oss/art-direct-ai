# ART DIRECTING AI — Visual Shot List

**Target:** 12 core visual examples for a ~40-page paid guide.

## Global art-direction rules for all GOOD examples

- Traditional children's-book illustration feel on warm off-white paper.
- Loose graphite / colored pencil / light watercolor or dry gouache.
- Minimum marks for maximum effect.
- Simple clothing shapes; no invented pattern, embroidery, lace, trim, or visible weave unless the lesson requires it.
- Hair treated as silhouette and broad masses, not individual strands.
- Sparse facial marks; no glossy eyes, cosmetic blush, rendered lips, or polished skin gradients.
- Backgrounds simplified into broad masses with intentional unfinished paper.
- No decorative filler merely to make a scene prettier.
- No cinematic rim lighting, glowing particles, bokeh, or magical dust unless the lesson explicitly demonstrates those problems.
- Imperfect edges and visible paper texture are welcome.
- Avoid fake handwritten text inside generated art. All labels/captions are added later in layout.

## BAD examples

Bad examples should be recognizably overworked, but still plausible enough that a beginner might initially say, “That looks good.” Do not turn them into parody. The teaching value comes from showing how technically impressive choices can still be wrong for the book.

## Visual 1 — The core before/after

**Chapter:** Introduction / Chapter 1

**Same scene:** A 7-year-old girl in a plain mustard-yellow dress walks through a small wooded area carrying a red balloon.

**BAD:** polished digital children's-art finish; glossy face; detailed individual hair; patterned or over-rendered dress; cinematic sun shafts; dense leaves; decorative wildflowers; balloon subtly glowing.

**GOOD:** loose graphite + colored pencil + faint watercolor; yellow dress is one plain shape with only a few posture folds; hair as a dark simple mass; three broad tree masses; large areas of visible paper; quiet red balloon; no glow.

**Lesson:** Same subject. Different behavior from the illustrator.

**Chapter:** Chapter 3

A clean art-direction reference board, assembled in layout from separate small illustrations rather than generated as a page with text.

- one hair silhouette treatment;
- one plain clothing treatment;

**Lesson:** A style is a system of repeated decisions, not one adjective.

## Visual 3 — Character reference sheet

**Chapter:** Chapter 5

Same child shown front, three-quarter, side, and back plus 3 restrained expressions.

Character:
- about 8 years old;
- medium-brown skin;
- simple dark curly bob silhouette ending near jaw;
- plain rust-red T-shirt;
- denim shorts simplified to flat shapes;
- simple sneakers.

Keep poses neutral and useful. No elaborate turnaround presentation generated as graphic design; isolated drawings will be arranged in layout.

**Lesson:** Lock identity before page one.

## Visual 4 — Composition and text-safe space

**Chapter:** Chapter 6

**Scene:** Boy kneeling beside a small cardboard robot on a bedroom floor.

Two compositions using same restrained style:

**BAD PAGE DESIGN:** boy and robot centered; important shapes everywhere; no safe place for 4–5 lines of text.

**GOOD PAGE DESIGN:** boy and robot occupy lower-left or lower-right third; upper opposite third mostly untouched warm paper/light wash; no important objects behind future type.

**Lesson:** A good picture is not automatically a usable page.

---

## Visual 5 — Character drift

**Chapter:** Chapter 5 / 7

Four small portraits intended to depict the same boy.

**BAD set:** age, head shape, hair length, eye treatment, shirt collar, and proportions subtly shift from image to image.

**GOOD set:** same boy remains identifiable while expression and head angle change.

**Lesson:** Consistency preserves identity without freezing expression.

---

## Visual 6 — Face restraint

**Chapter:** Chapter 8

Same girl's face at similar angle.

**BAD:** glossy catchlights, rendered eyelashes, airbrushed skin, blush gradient, detailed lips, perfectly symmetrical polished finish.

**GOOD:** sparse pencil features, simple eyes without glassy reflections, skin mostly flat wash, slight asymmetry, only enough information to read the expression.

**Lesson:** Describe how much face the book allows.

---

## Visual 7 — Hair hierarchy

**Chapter:** Chapter 9

Same child with curly hair.

**BAD:** hundreds of individual curls/strands, highlights, flyaways, rim light, complex internal texture.

**GOOD:** full culturally accurate volume and silhouette preserved; internal texture suggested with a few grouped marks; no strand-by-strand rendering.

**Lesson:** Simplification does not mean erasing hair identity.

---

## Visual 8 — The fabric test

**Chapter:** Chapter 10

Same girl standing naturally in a yellow dress.

**BAD:** tiny floral repeat, lace/trim, realistic folds, visible stitching, fabric highlights, subtle weave, unnecessary decorative waist detail.

**GOOD:** plain mustard-yellow dress; simple neckline and sleeves; 2–4 folds only where needed to explain pose; no pattern or embellishment.

**Caption concept for layout:** “Would a human illustrator really spend this much time on the dress?”

**Lesson:** The generator does not feel the labor cost of detail. You must.

---

## Visual 9 — Everything is important

**Chapter:** Chapter 11

**Scene:** Child secretly gives a stray cat food under a kitchen table.

**BAD:** child/cat plus highly rendered patterned curtains, shelf objects, tiles, jars, plants, fruit, shiny cookware, decorative rug.

**GOOD:** child and cat clearly resolved; table legs and bowl readable; kitchen reduced to broad low-contrast shapes; large quiet areas.

**Lesson:** Detail functions like a spotlight.

---

## Visual 10 — Glow inflation

**Chapter:** Chapter 12

**Scene:** Child reading an ordinary library book beside a bedroom window at dusk.

**BAD:** book emits golden glow, rim light around child, floating particles, sparkling dust, cinematic beams.

**GOOD:** ordinary ambient dusk/window light and small lamp if needed; book is simply a book; emotion comes from posture and expression.

**Lesson:** Emotional importance does not automatically mean luminous effects.

---

## Visual 11 — Decorative clutter / continuity debt

**Chapter:** Chapter 13

**Scene:** recurring child's bedroom.

**BAD location:** plant, string lights, posters, patterned pillows, multiple toys, shelves, wall art, blanket patterns, desk accessories, decorative objects everywhere.

**GOOD location:** only established anchors: low wooden bed, blue blanket, white nightstand, red lamp, two framed child drawings, worn rug; everything else quiet.

**Lesson:** Every invented object is another object you may have to reproduce later.

---

## Visual 12 — Full worked example sequence

**Chapter:** Chapter 18

**Manuscript beat:** “Milo sat on the floor beside the cardboard robot. One wheel had fallen off again.”

Assets:
1. weak/default output;
2. restrained character reference for Milo;
3. simple robot reference;
4. corrected page composition with text-safe space;
5. improved art-directed output;
6. final edited/cropped spread-ready image.

Milo:
- about 8;
- medium-brown skin;
- simple dark curly hair mass;
- plain faded green T-shirt;
- dark shorts;
- simple socks;
- no logos or patterns.

Robot:
- homemade cardboard box body;
- two bottle-cap eyes;
- simple antenna;
- mismatched small wheels;
- visibly handmade, not cute polished sci-fi design.

Room:
- floor and a few broad bedroom cues only;
- no decorative clutter;
- generous blank area reserved for manuscript text.

**Lesson:** Show the entire system from manuscript line through final page.

# Production rule

Never approve an example merely because it looks attractive.

For every GOOD image ask:

**Does this image visibly demonstrate the restraint we are charging the reader to learn?**

If not, regenerate or simplify it.
