# Art Direct AI — Pass 5 Typography & Grid System
Date: 2026-09-05
Status: locked for first 48-page proof

## Production principle
The final guide must look designed, not generated.

Illustration generation supplies/repairs art assets. All final headings, body copy, captions, labels, checklists, measurements, page numbers, rules, arrows, diagrams, and workflow text are built normally in layout.

## Page geometry
- Final interior: 48 pages.
- Work as flat pages/spreads, never photographed-book mockups.
- Use one consistent outer margin system.
- Keep generous inside protection near binding.
- Exact trim/bleed/safe values remain destination-dependent until printer/platform verification.
- Do not bake unverified measurements into artwork.

## Grid
Use a simple flexible 6-column page grid.

Default patterns:
1. **Editorial page:** 2 columns text + 4 columns art, or inverse.
2. **Comparison page:** 3 + 3 columns.
3. **Reference/checklist page:** full-width header, then 2-column working area.
4. **Quiet page:** break the grid intentionally; large raw-paper field.
5. **Spread lesson:** 12-column combined spread logic while protecting the center.
6. **Opener/closing:** minimal grid use, one dominant idea.

Do not fill every grid cell.

## Typography hierarchy

### Display / chapter title
- Traditional editorial serif.
- Strong, bookish, human.
- Avoid geometric tech/startup display faces.
- Sentence/title case rather than UI-style all caps except small chapter kicker.
- Typical visual target: 30–42 pt depending on page.

### Section heading
- Same serif family, bold/semibold.
- Typical visual target: 18–24 pt.

### Body
- Highly readable serif.
- Typical visual target: 10.5–12 pt with comfortable leading.
- Short paragraphs.
- Avoid tiny body copy to preserve generated-layout density.

### Utility labels
- Compact sans serif or small caps serif.
- Used sparingly for BEFORE / AFTER / CHECK / LOCKED / FLEXIBLE.
- Typical visual target: 8.5–10 pt.

### Pull quote / rule
- Serif, larger than body.
- Give it air.
- Do not put every takeaway in a colored card.

## Type behavior
- Ragged-right body preferred where practical.
- Avoid tight justified columns.
- No generated text embedded in final art.
- No fake handwriting font used to simulate authenticity.
- Avoid excessive bold.
- Use italics for brief emphasis only.
- Keep line lengths comfortable; do not stretch body text across full landscape spreads.

## Color system
Use the source-comp family as accents, not as a dashboard system:
- warm off-white paper base;
- navy/dark ink for primary typography;
- muted blue;
- muted green;
- muted rose;
- muted ochre/yellow;
- occasional muted violet.

Rules:
- one dominant accent per lesson/page;
- pale tints only when a teaching distinction benefits;
- green/red reserved mainly for genuine good/bad comparisons;
- no rainbow sequence merely because six steps exist;
- large areas should remain warm paper.

## Rules, boxes, and panels
- Prefer open composition over containers.
- Use thin rules and light tint fields only when they clarify grouping.
- Avoid rounded SaaS cards.
- Avoid drop shadows.
- Avoid glossy gradients.
- Avoid dashboard tiles.
- Avoid excessive checkboxes when prose or a short list teaches better.

## Illustration treatment
- Preserve warm paper and visible grain.
- Do not digitally over-clean approved loose art.
- Crop generated typography away where possible.
- If art must be isolated, preserve imperfect pencil edges.
- Do not add cinematic vignette, glow, or digital texture.
- Avoid decorative frames unless the lesson specifically needs a boundary.

## Captions
Captions explain what to notice, not what the image already shows.

Good:
"Same scene. The second version gives the background less visual authority."

Weak:
"Boy and dog sitting by a lake."

Keep captions short.

## Comparison pages
For BEFORE/AFTER or GOOD/BAD:
- make the visual difference obvious at normal page size;
- keep art panels large;
- use real labels outside the image;
- do not depend on tiny annotations;
- bad examples may be intentionally broken but must not accidentally become the visual hero.

## Checklists
- Real vector checkbox/checkmark glyphs.
- Comfortable row spacing.
- Maximum useful density, not maximum possible density.
- Split across pages rather than shrinking type.

## Diagrams
Draw trim, bleed, safe-zone, gutter, arrows, workflow paths, and measurements as vector/layout objects.
Never ask image generation to render exact production diagrams.

## Page numbers
- quiet, consistent footer placement;
- omit where a full-bleed/opening treatment genuinely benefits;
- never use generated page numbers from source art.

## Spread seam
The final digital PDF is flat artwork.
Do not simulate:
- center fold shadow;
- paper curl;
- photographed gutter;
- book-edge thickness.

The only center indicator allowed is an instructional guide when the lesson explicitly teaches gutter/binding.

## Restraint test for every assembled page
Before approval ask:
1. Is the story/lesson obvious first?
2. Is any panel present because the generator happened to make it?
3. Can one box, rule, caption, icon, or decorative mark be removed?
4. Does the illustration still feel loose after layout?
5. Does the page look like a book—or a presentation/dashboard?
6. Is there enough untouched paper?

If removing something improves the page, remove it.

## Proof-build order
Build in this order:
1. pages 1–5 — establish visual system;
2. pages 6–15 — diagnostic/detail lessons;
3. pages 16–25 — character block;
4. pages 26–33 — composition/page turns;
5. pages 34–42 — iteration/prompting;
6. pages 43–48 — production/closing;
7. whole-book rhythm pass.

Do not start by perfecting page 45 diagrams before the visual system is proven on pages 1–5.
