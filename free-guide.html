MISTAKE 1

The “Make Me a Children's Book Illustration” Problem

A vague prompt asks the model to make all the creative decisions for you. That's exactly when it falls back on familiar, polished defaults.

Typical prompt

“A cute little boy and his dog in a forest, children's book illustration.”

What the model decides: lighting, medium, rendering level, composition, texture, palette, facial style, and background density.

Better direction

“Loose pencil and restrained watercolor on warm textured paper. Simple shapes. Quiet background. Limited detail. Avoid digital polish, glowing effects, glossy eyes, and hyper-rendered hair.”

You decide: what matters, what stays quiet, and what the image should not become.

A useful prompt doesn't just describe the scene. It describes how the illustrator should behave.

MISTAKE 2

Too Much Detail

Generative models often interpret “beautiful,” “professional,” or “magical” as more: more hair, more texture, more lighting, more flowers, more fabric pattern, more everything.

Over-rendered

  • Individual hair strands
  • Glossy eyes and perfect skin
  • Complex fabric patterns
  • Decorative foliage everywhere
  • Cinematic rim lighting
  • Background objects added just to fill space

Restrained

  • Hair treated as a few simple masses
  • Plain clothing unless story-relevant
  • Soft natural texture
  • Background simplified into large shapes
  • Open areas left unfinished
  • Detail concentrated near the story beat
If a real illustrator would need three extra hours to render a dress pattern nobody asked for, tell the model not to put it there.

MISTAKE 3 + 4

No Style System — and Characters That Mutate

Repeating the same style words on every page does not create consistency. You need references and a short list of visual rules that stay fixed.

Without a system

The same character slowly changes:

  • Age and face shape
  • Hair length and color
  • Shirt color and accessories
  • Head-to-body proportions
  • Rendering style

Build a character sheet first

Front, side, and back. Then lock the details that cannot change.

Think like an art director, not a prompt machine. Build the visual language before you build the book.

PAGE 4 • REFERENCES BEAT REPEATING THE SAME ADJECTIVES

MISTAKE 5

AI Tries Too Hard

Sometimes the strongest instruction is simply to stop trying to make every frame spectacular.

“Make it beautiful”

  • Dramatic sunset
  • Lens-like lighting
  • Perfect composition
  • Decorative effects
  • Every corner finished

“Make it right for this page”

  • Simple composition
  • Natural color
  • One clear emotional beat
  • Room for typography
  • Quiet areas and imperfect marks
